How do I find my typical views?

Sponsors pay for the people who see their video, so you need the views of one normal video.

  1. Open your stats (on YouTube: YouTube Studio → Content, the Views column).
  2. Write down the views of your last 9 videos. Skip very new ones that are still growing.
  3. Put them in order, smallest to biggest, and take the middle one (the 5th).

Why the middle and not the average? One viral video pulls the average up, and a sponsor will judge you by your normal videos. The middle number (the "median") ignores the one-off hit.

Why not my yearly views?

1.3M views in a year sounds big, but the sponsor's ad appears in one video. If you posted 52 videos, that's about 25,000 views per video, and that's the number they pay for.

Yearly views ÷ number of videos gives you a rough average. The middle-video method above is more honest.

No past deals yet?

If you're in the YouTube Partner Program, check your RPM in YouTube Studio → Analytics → Revenue. RPM is what YouTube pays you per 1,000 views from ads.

A sponsor usually pays more than that, because their message is part of your video. Treat your RPM as a minimum, not a target.

Several past deals?

Work out the rate for each one, then compare. Use the most recent, or the middle one if they're very different. Your rate is a decision, not a law: you can raise it as your channel grows.
